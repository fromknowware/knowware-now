## What the Department alleges

The following is the Department of Justice's account, as described in its November 24, 2025 release [[1]](#src-1). It is an allegation.

- RealPage's revenue management software has relied on nonpublic, competitively sensitive information shared by landlords to set rental prices.
- The software has included features designed to limit rental price decreases and otherwise align pricing among competitors.
- RealPage has hosted meetings attended by competing property management companies where competitively sensitive information was shared.

The Department places the case within its ongoing enforcement against algorithmic coordination, information sharing and other anticompetitive practices in rental housing markets. Its release quotes Assistant Attorney General Abigail Slater of the Antitrust Division saying that competing companies must make independent pricing decisions, and that the Department will remain at the forefront of vigorous antitrust enforcement as algorithmic and artificial intelligence tools rise [[1]](#src-1).

## What the proposed settlement would require

If the court approves it, the proposed consent judgment would require RealPage to [[1]](#src-1):

- Stop having its software use competitors' nonpublic, competitively sensitive information to determine rental prices in runtime operation.
- Stop using active lease data to train the underlying models, limiting training to historic nonpublic data aged at least 12 months.
- Not use models that determine geographic effects narrower than the state level.
- Remove or redesign features that limited price decreases or aligned pricing between competing users.
- Stop conducting market surveys to collect competitively sensitive information, and refrain from discussing market analyses, trends or pricing strategies based on nonpublic data in its revenue management meetings.
- Accept a court-appointed monitor, and cooperate in the United States' lawsuit against property management companies that have used its software.

Under the Tunney Act the proposed settlement and a competitive impact statement are published in the Federal Register for a 60-day public comment period, after which the U.S. District Court for the Middle District of North Carolina may enter final judgment if it finds the settlement in the public interest [[1]](#src-1).

## Where the third party was missing

The release describes landlords and property managers sharing data with the software and attending RealPage's meetings. Renters, whose prices the software is alleged to have influenced, are not described as part of any of it. The Department states its aim as restoring free market competition in rental markets for millions of American renters [[1]](#src-1). Whether the conduct alleged was unlawful has not been decided in anything this entry cites.
