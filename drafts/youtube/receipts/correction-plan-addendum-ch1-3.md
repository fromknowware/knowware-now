## 1. Ch. 1, Facebook: the manipulation was omission (new Tier 1 row; companion to existing items F and G)

| Where | Now says | Source says | Proposed fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ch. 1 opening section (full text `chapter-01.md`, "The $50 Million Algorithm...") | "some users saw significantly more positive content, while others were exposed to an increased amount of negative content" | Kramer, Guillory, Hancock, PNAS 111(24):8788–8790. One group had friends' positive posts left out of their News Feed and another had negative posts left out, each emotional post having a 10% to 90% chance of omission per load; controls had posts omitted at random. No group was shown added content. (1A receipt 3, body ¶5–6.) | "For one week in January 2012, some users had a share of their friends' positive posts left out of their News Feed, and others had a share of the negative posts left out." |

Related points already in the plan, with the new evidence for them:
- **G ("proved, unequivocally"):** the paper itself writes "first" / "some of the first" experimental evidence, calls the effects small ("as small as d = 0.001"), and measures the share of positive and negative words in users' own posts, not mood (1A receipts 5, 7, 8).
- **G (consent):** the paper asserts consent through the Data Use Policy (receipt 6); the PNAS Expression of Concern (July 3, 2014) calls consent a "matter of concern" and says practices "may" not have been fully consistent with informed consent (receipt 9). State both, not one.
- **F (UCSF):** confirmed. UCSF is only Guillory's present address (receipt 11); Cornell's June 30, 2014 statement also says "now at UCSF".
- **O ("$50 Million"):** nothing in the paper, the Expression of Concern or Cornell's statement mentions a dollar figure (1A section 4).

Also in that paragraph:
- **"Seven people died":** confirmed. "All seven crew members perished" (Vol. I Ch. III, opening paragraph; receipt 1). No change needed. Launch was January 28, 1986, 11:38 a.m. EST, and the breakup came 73 seconds later.
- **Feynman's demonstration, "proved this":** Feynman's own statement was narrower: material from the seal, put in ice water, showed no resilience at 32°F (receipt 11). Describe it as what he said at the hearing, not as something that "proved" the paragraph's thesis about coordination.
- **"A glass of ice water and a piece of rubber":** the Feb 11, 1986 transcript has "ice water" and the seal material but no glass or clamp. The clamp-in-a-glass remark is a separate question to Hardy on Feb 13, 1986 (Vol. IV p. 952). Either cite the transcript wording or verify the "glass" against video or another primary source before keeping it.
- **Where the demonstration is documented (a correction to the brief and slate, not to the book):** Vol. IV hearing transcript, Feb 11, 1986, pp. 679–680 (`nasa.gov/history/rogersrep/v4part4.htm`). It is **not** in Appendix F, and I found no description of it in Vol. I. If the author wants to cite the Commission's report for Feynman, Appendix F's actual content is the 1-in-100 versus 1-in-100,000 gap between engineers' and managers' failure estimates (receipt 12).
